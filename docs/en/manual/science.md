# Scientific concepts, units and convergence

This chapter explains the physical meaning of each kind of PyRPMD result. Decide first which
quantity you want, then choose the task, potential and sampling method; that keeps free energies,
sampling time, reaction rates and diffusion coefficients from being confused. Inputs and commands
are in the [workflow guide](workflows.md), [Periodic materials and surfaces](periodic.md) and
[PCET](pcet.md).

## Choosing the scientific question

| Goal | Task | Main result and normalization |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thermal rate of two gas-phase reactants | `rate` | PMF, transmission coefficient and bimolecular rate in `cm^3 molecule^-1 s^-1` |
| Launch two reactants explicitly and classify products | `drpmd` | Thermal rate or fixed-energy cross section, depending on the initial distribution |
| Equilibrium free energy along chosen CVs | `fes` | Probability density with respect to the CV volume element, and its free energy |
| Separate rates for several entrances and TSs | `network` | Independent rate matrix per entrance and TS |
| Explore 1D/2D CVs with a history-dependent bias | `metadynamics` | Finite-time bias free-energy estimate and HILLS |
| Equilibrium nuclear quantum statistics in a fixed cell | `pimd` | Physical energy estimators, ring radius of gyration |
| Thermostat-free RPMD transport after equilibration | `diffusion` | Centroid MSD, VACF, diffusion tensor and diagnostics |
| A selected exit from a material site or surface basin | `site_rate` / `surface_rate` | Basin-normalized first-order rate in `s^-1` |
| Local electrochemical weakly coupled proton–electron transfer | `pcet` | Reduction/oxidation model rates and isotope effects in `s^-1` |

`pcet` uses one-dimensional proton eigenstates and a golden-rule rate expression; it does not
propagate RPMD. All other dynamics tasks share one real-atomic-mass ring-polymer convention.
`network` currently supports only `aggregation="separate"`; fluxes through different TSs are not
added automatically into a total rate.

## Atomic units and array conventions

Computations use atomic units internally. In the low-level Python API, numbers are already in
atomic units; input files accept dimension-checked strings with units. Do not rely on geometry
files and inline arrays having the same default unit.

| Quantity | Internal unit / shape | Recommended input |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Positions, cell | bohr; `q[P,N,3]`, `cell[3,3]` | `geometry_unit="angstrom"`, `cell_unit="angstrom"` |
| Energy | hartree | `"0.7 eV"`, `"1 kcal/mol"` |
| Force | hartree/bohr, the negative gradient of the physical potential | PES output `[B,N,3]` |
| Mass | electron masses | `masses_amu` or isotope masses with `amu` |
| Time | atomic time | `"0.1 fs"`, `"2 ps"` |
| Temperature | kelvin on input; internally `beta=1/(k_B T)` | `temperature="300 K"` |
| Momentum | atomic units, `p[P,N,3]` | Generated by the sampler from mass and temperature |
| PES batch | coordinates `[B,N,3]`, energies `[B]` | B is the number of frames in this call, not necessarily the total bead count |
| D-dimensional CV | `value[D]`, `jacobian[D,P,N,3]` | A custom CV must return the full ring-coordinate Jacobian |

All atom indices start at 0. XYZ coordinates default to angstrom; inline coordinates default to
bohr. Each row of a cell is a lattice vector and `r = s @ cell`; boundary conventions are detailed
in [Periodic structures](periodic.md).

A potential returns the **physical potential energy and physical forces** of each bead. The
propagator adds the ring springs; a PES must not add springs, divide by the bead number or add the
umbrella again. Unit conversion, error handling and the optional virial of the interface are in
the [potential interface specification](https://github.com/chen-yu-hao/PyRPMD/blob/main/docs/potentials.md) *(partly Chinese)*.

## Bead number and the ring-polymer Hamiltonian

For P beads and N atoms PyRPMD uses the summed Hamiltonian

$$
H_P=\sum_{j=0}^{P-1}\left[
\sum_i\frac{|p_{ji}|^2}{2m_i}+V(q_j)
+\sum_i\frac{m_i\omega_P^2}{2}|q_{ji}-q_{j-1,i}|^2
\right],\qquad \omega_P=\frac{P}{\beta}.
$$

With $\hbar=1$ in atomic units the equilibrium distribution is $\exp[-(\beta/P)H_P]$, so the
Maxwell variance of one momentum component is $m_iP/\beta$. For P=1 there are no ring springs and
deterministic propagation reduces to classical molecular dynamics. Increasing P tests the
path-integral discretization; it does not add independent experimental samples.

The centroid is $\bar q_i=P^{-1}\sum_j q_{ji}$. For a nonlinear function s the two CVs below
generally differ:

$$
s_{\mathrm{centroid}}=s(\bar q),\qquad
s_{\mathrm{bead\ average}}=\frac{1}{P}\sum_j s(q_j).
$$

Choose `estimator="centroid"` or `"bead_average"` according to the physical quantity of interest.
The Jacobian of a centroid CV already contains the factor $1/P$; a custom plugin must neither omit
nor duplicate it.

If the physical umbrella is $W(s)=\tfrac12(s-c)^TK(s-c)$, the summed Hamiltonian receives $P W$
and the bias force on the full ring is $-P J^T K(s-c)$. This is why the reduced MBAR bias of the
generic FES is $\beta W$ and not multiplied by P again. When length, angle and other CVs are mixed,
the components of the center and stiffness matrix must have matching dimensions.

## Sampling versus real-time propagation

PIMD, umbrella windows and constrained parent chains exist to generate a required equilibrium
distribution. Their thermostatted dynamics time is not an experimental transport or reaction time.
dRPMD collisions, RPMD recrossing children and the production stage of `diffusion` use
thermostat-free, bias-free deterministic propagation.

| Deterministic integrator | Free-ring treatment | Errors to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `cayley-bcb` | Cayley free-ring map, the default | Finite-step error of the physical force and the splitting |
| `normal-mode-bab` | Exact normal-mode free-ring rotation | Physical-force and splitting error; an exact free ring does not make the full dynamics exact |
| `velocity-verlet` | Explicit springs and physical forces | Stability limit of the highest ring frequency and stricter limits from the physical potential |

Every method needs a time-step reduction study of the target result. The necessary free-ring
stability condition for Verlet is $|\Delta t|\max\omega_k<2$; passing it does not guarantee
stability with stiff bonds or stiff biases. Cayley free-ring stability is not an accuracy
guarantee for arbitrarily large steps either. See the
[integrator notes](https://github.com/chen-yu-hao/PyRPMD/blob/main/docs/integrators.md).

Ordinary reaction and FES workflows use Andersen sampling matched to the chosen integrator.
Materials `pimd` / `diffusion` thermalization additionally supports PILE-L: `bcocb` for Cayley,
`baoab` for the normal-mode integrator, and Andersen for Verlet. `native=true` allows compiled
substeps; `loop_backend="native"` requires the whole fused propagation loop to be compatible; the
two mean different things. Restrictions for periodic/fixed-atom systems and PILE-L are in the
[periodic task guide](periodic.md).

## Free-energy measures and rate normalization

The generic FES is a density free energy on the volume element of the chosen CV:

$$
F(s)=-\beta^{-1}\log\rho(s)+C.
$$

A histogram probability mass must be divided by the bin volume to become a density. A coordinate
transformation changes the density measure, so the geometric degeneracy contained in a distance
FES is not removed automatically. A barrier on a 2D free-energy surface does not have a unique
rate prefactor either.

The gas-phase `rate` uses a dedicated PMF with a flux-mass metric and a bimolecular normalization
of two distant fragments. Local site rates in materials and surfaces use a position density and an
explicit basin probability and report a first-order rate. In both cases the PMF, denominator and
units belong together; you cannot swap only the prefactor. See [gas-phase rates](workflows.md) and
[basin rates](periodic.md).

The physical energies reported by `pimd` also differ from the ring Hamiltonian. When all atoms are
mobile, the potential-energy estimate is the bead average of the physical potential, and the
centroid-virial kinetic energy is

$$
K_{\mathrm{cv}}=\frac{3N}{2\beta}
-\frac{1}{2P}\sum_{ji}(q_{ji}-\bar q_i)\cdot F_{ji}.
$$

The physical internal energy estimate is $\langle V\rangle_P+K_{\mathrm{cv}}$. The instantaneous
kinetic energy of the fictitious ring momenta is not used as the quantum physical kinetic energy;
`ring_polymer_hamiltonian` is a propagation diagnostic. The current materials estimators require
all atoms to be mobile.

## Deciding whether a result can be trusted

A successful run only shows that the given input finished. A physical conclusion also depends on
the suitability of the potential, the reaction definition, the discretization parameters and
finite sampling. Organize validation in the following layers and keep the complete input and
result of every change:

1. **Model and interface.** Check elements, isotopes, masses, charge, spin and the PES definition;
   finite-difference the forces. For periodic systems also check the cell, periodic translations
   and partial PBC.
2. **Numerical parameters.** Vary the bead number, time step and relevant grid/bin settings
   separately and compare the energy, free energy, rate or D you care about, not only whether the
   trajectory diverged.
3. **Equilibration and coverage.** Increase thermalization, independent replicas and production
   length; check slow CV modes, window overlap, effective sample sizes and the support of basins
   and dividing surfaces. Relative free energies between disconnected windows are not identifiable.
4. **Task-specific conditions.** In the gas phase, check `rinf` / the initial separation, `bmax`,
   classification and the maximum collision time; for local rates, the basin and the recrossing
   plateau; for diffusion, the long-time linear regime, reference frame, size and fitting window;
   for PCET, the coordinate domain, grid, number of states and the weak-coupling model.
5. **Statistical errors.** Use independent replicas or block/parent bootstraps that keep
   correlations. Overlapping time origins, several trajectories launched from one parent chain and
   the beads of one ring cannot simply be treated as independent samples.

An error bar describes the statistical uncertainty of the sampled problem; it usually excludes
potential-model error, unvisited slow modes, finite size and bead-number error. A missing error
must stay `null`; unsampled bins must stay masked. Do not interpret a missing value as zero or fill
it in before reporting a rate. The upper and lower bounds from unresolved dRPMD trajectories are not
statistical confidence intervals either.

The program keeps `converged=false` or `physical_converged=false`; PCET additionally reports
`numerical_convergence` and `physical_applicability_unverified`. These flags separate "the
calculation finished", "a specific numerical check passed" and "the physical prediction is
converged". For theory and implementation details continue with the
[scientific conventions](https://github.com/chen-yu-hao/PyRPMD/blob/main/docs/science.md) *(Chinese)*,
the [MBAR rate PMF](https://github.com/chen-yu-hao/PyRPMD/blob/main/docs/mbar-rate-pmf.md) *(Chinese)*
and the method sources cited in each task topic.
